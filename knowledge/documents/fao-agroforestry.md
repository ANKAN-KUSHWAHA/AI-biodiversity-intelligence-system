---
Agroforestry deliberately combines woody perennials with crops and/or livestock. It can increase structural diversity in agricultural landscapes and can provide habitat, resources and connectivity for biodiversity when designed appropriately. Trees can also influence microclimate, soil cover and water processes, although competition for water may be important in water-limited areas.

Species selection, spacing, management and local rainfall conditions determine the trade-offs. Agroforestry is therefore a design choice that needs local assessment rather than a universal prescription.
